01 / APPROACH

From prediction
to its conditions.

A useful biological prior is not necessarily a universal one.

Structural similarity, evolutionary history, and biological context can guide learning. But a relationship that is informative in one setting may be misleading in another. I am interested in making those conditions explicit, rather than treating better prediction as sufficient explanation.

My working process is to identify an assumption, look for counterexamples, and design comparisons that separate biological signal from shortcuts or confounding.

01

Protein representations for enzyme function

MSc thesis · Completed / follow-up in development

When does structural similarity support functional transfer?

My MSc thesis examined the relationship between protein structural similarity and consistency of enzyme commission (EC) annotations. Working with ProtT5, ProstT5, Foldseek, and CLEAN, I explored how sequence- and structure-informed representations relate to enzyme function prediction.

Supervised by Ole Winther at the Technical University of Denmark. Follow-up questions focus on identifying when structural information is useful—and when it is not.

02

Confounding in polygenic height prediction

Research presentation · ISMB/ECCB 2025 · VarI

What is a nonlinear predictor learning beyond the target signal?

This work examines sex-related confounding in nonlinear polygenic height prediction, using representation disentanglement and statistical controls to interrogate predictive behavior. The central concern is whether a model's representation reflects the intended genetic signal or a related source of variation.

Presented as an oral flash presentation in the VarI track at ISMB/ECCB 2025; listed here as a presentation, not a journal publication.

03

Interpreting protein domain co-occurrence

Past collaborative contribution · Analysis

Does occurring together imply being compatible?

My contribution focused on interpreting domain co-occurrence through frequency baselines and cluster-level generalization. An observed association may reflect how often individual domains appear, or which protein families are represented, rather than a transferable compatibility relationship.

This perspective motivates separating descriptive patterns from claims about unseen combinations.

04

Evaluating text-to-protein generation

Ongoing research direction · Evaluation

What would count as evidence that a generated protein meets its description?

I am exploring how to evaluate foldability, alignment with intended function, and the independence of evaluation signals. A convincing assessment should distinguish a plausible structure from evidence for the requested biological behavior.

This is a developing direction, not a claim of experimentally validated generation.

03 / CURRENT AGENDA

The next questions.

A research agenda—not a list of solved problems.

I.

Boundaries of transfer

Under which conditions can a structural or evolutionary relationship justify transferring functional information to an unfamiliar protein?

II.

Anticipating useful priors

Can properties observable on the training side help predict when structural information will improve inference on new examples?

III.

Evidence beyond a score

How can controlled model comparisons and uncertainty estimates reveal whether an apparent gain reflects biology, confounding, or evaluation design?
